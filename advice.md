## A. Why it's still a variations model, not a generator

**1. Occupancy is modeled as a continuous channel denoised by Gaussian MSE — your structure prior is a 1-D regression.** Your state is `[offset(3), intensity(1), class_probs(8), mask(1)]`, and "where does geometry exist" is decided entirely by the `mask` scalar, denoised with plain MSE (`geom_pred` in `SparseDiffusion.forward`) and thresholded at 0 in `remove_mask`. This is the crux. In a single-exemplar model you can get away with it because the grid *is* the exemplar and occupancy barely varies; in a dataset model the occupancy field **is** the distribution you're trying to learn. XCube (Ren et al., CVPR 2024) — your stated reference — deliberately does not do this: it diffuses a latent and models the sparse structure / subdivision explicitly per level, so the coarse stage learns p(structure) as a first-class object. Treating occupancy as one more Gaussian channel, decoupled from semantics by a separate loss head, is exactly the decoupling you diagnosed earlier (geometry persists but mislabels). After `to_custom_dense`, mask is −1 on the overwhelming majority of voxels, so the MSE-optimal mask score drifts toward "−1 everywhere" → your level-0 occupancy saturation is the predictable equilibrium, not a tuning artifact.

**2. The class field is Gaussian-diffused but CE-supervised — the labels you sample aren't drawn from a coherent generative model.** You diffuse 8 continuous class channels, supervise with cross-entropy against soft targets, then `softmax` only at readout (`_sigmoid_semantic_channels`, which despite the name applies softmax). During the reverse loop those channels are updated by the **Gaussian** posterior, not by any simplex-respecting transition. So you're stapling a CE-trained readout onto a Gaussian transition kernel — the categorical marginal is never modeled as categorical. The principled options are discrete diffusion (D3PM, Austin et al. 2021), analog bits (Chen et al. 2023), or diffusing a continuous latent that *decodes* to classes (the LDM/XCube pattern, Rombach et al. 2022). Until labels and occupancy live in one coherent generative state, they'll keep behaving independently.

**3. You never train on the distribution you sample from.** This is subtle but decisive. `load_crop_level0` clips a `clip_size`-cube (≤~40³) **centered on a randomly chosen occupied voxel**, then densifies. Inference (`compute_canonical_base_grid`) denoises the *entire* 16²×8 / 64²×8 tile in one shot, borders and all. So at train time every window is occupancy-centered and patch-sized; at test time you ask for a full-tile global layout the model has never been shown. That is still internal-patch learning — the SinGAN / Sin3DM / SinDDM regime (Shaham et al. 2019; Wu et al. 2024; Kulikov et al. 2023) — where you learn local/patch statistics and resynthesize, not a tile-level layout prior p(scene). To learn mesoscopic marginals you have to present full (or at least uncentered, occupancy-agnostic) coarse tiles during training, and eat the memory cost — or move to a sparser latent so the full tile fits. Relatedly, `to_custom_dense` fills empty voxels with *blurred neighbor features* (mask=−1), so "empty" voxels carry smoothed signal at train time but start from pure `randn` at inference — a train/test mismatch on precisely the voxels whose mask decides structure.

**4. The conditioning hook is still dead, so there's no controllable-generation path.** `forward(self, x, t, cond=None)` ignores `cond` in both `DiffusionCNN` and `DiffusionUNet`. For a dataset generator you need either (a) a strong *unconditional* layout prior — blocked by items 1–3 — or (b) coarse-level conditioning on a scene-graph/layout latent (your GVAE) via FiLM or cross-attention with classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans 2022). Neither is wired, so the only coherent scene the model can emit is one implied by the grid you hand it. That is the mechanical definition of "variations."

## B. Diffusion fundamentals that are mis-specified

**5. Noise conditioning is drastically under-resolved and injected only once.** `time_emb=6`. `sinusoidal_embedding` with dim 6 gives half_dim 3, frequencies [π, 2π, 4π], over t∈[0,1] — the highest frequency completes *two cycles across the entire trajectory*. The denoiser essentially cannot resolve adjacent noise levels. Every reference implementation (DDPM, Ho et al. 2020; Improved DDPM, Nichol & Dhariwal 2021; ADM, Dhariwal & Nichol 2021; EDM, Karras et al. 2022) uses a 128–256-D Fourier/positional embedding through an MLP. Worse, you **concatenate** the 6 time channels to the input once at the stem; after the first stride-2 downsample that per-voxel signal is spatially averaged away, and the bottleneck/decoder see no σ at all. The score ∇ₓ log p(x;σ) depends *explicitly and strongly* on σ — a network that only sees σ at the input learns a σ-averaged score, which manifests as blurry, mode-collapsed samples. The fix is the standard one: a high-dim time embedding → MLP, broadcast into **every** residual block as FiLM (scale+shift). A single-exemplar model can memorize its way around weak time conditioning; a population model cannot.

**6. No residual blocks; LayerNorm-everywhere is a symptom-treatment.** Your encoder/decoder blocks are plain conv→LN→SiLU with no in-block skip. Diffusion U-Nets are built from residual blocks (He et al. 2016, adopted wholesale by DDPM/ADM) precisely because the network predicts a small correction to a noisy input — residual paths make the near-identity map trivial and stabilize deep score nets. Your own docstring says the LN is there to stop "gradient explosions"; that's the missing residual path talking. And per-voxel LayerNorm discards activation scale, which at high σ *is* the signal — ADM uses GroupNorm + time-FiLM for exactly this reason.

**7. Unweighted x0-MSE gives an *uncontrolled* noise-level weighting — replace it with a deliberate, bounded one.** `forward` draws t ~ U(0, ·) and applies unweighted MSE. **(Correction to an earlier version of this item, which had the sign backwards for an x0-predictor.)** I previously claimed this "under-trains the high-noise regime"; that imported ε-prediction intuition into an x0 setting where the implicit weighting is inverted. Your code predicts `x_start` with unweighted MSE, and since `‖Δx₀‖² = ‖Δε‖²/SNR`, the effective ε-space weight of your current loss is `1/SNR` (≈6.08 at SNR=0.01, →~0 by SNR=10). So realized gradients are *already* high-σ-dominated; if anything it's low-noise detail that's starved. The honest framing is therefore not "I need more high-σ emphasis" — you're already there, with an uncontrolled, near-divergent profile. The real win is swapping that implicit `1/SNR` for a *bounded, deliberate* curve.

Two caveats on the fixes:

- **Currency matters — don't drop P2 in naively.** P2 weighting (Choi et al. 2022), `(k+SNR)^(−γ)`, is published as a multiplier on the **ε-loss**, not the x0-loss. Multiplying your x0-MSE by it stacks an extra `1/SNR` (effective ε-weight becomes `(k+SNR)^(−γ)/SNR`), which barely changes the raw profile and is *not* the P2 objective. To run actual P2 on an x0-predictor the multiplier on your x0-MSE must be `w_x0(t) = SNR(t)·(k + SNR(t))^(−γ)`. General rule: to realize any desired ε-currency weight `w̃(σ)` from an x0-predictor, set the x0-loss multiplier to `SNR·w̃` (for ε-predictors it's `w̃` directly; for v, divide by `1+1/SNR`). This is also why Min-SNR-γ `min(SNR,γ)` *looks* like it suppresses high-σ in x0-space yet *raises* it to a flat ceiling in ε-space.
- **It's a second-order knob.** P2-true keeps the high-σ lean while taming the SNR→0 tail; Min-SNR-γ (Hang et al. 2023) rebalances toward mid-σ. Which wins is empirical — given the layout-failure symptom I'd bet on P2-true — but loss reweighting only *reallocates existing capacity across σ*. It cannot synthesize a layout prior the representation forbids; with occupancy as a regressed Gaussian channel and level-0 training on occupancy-centered clips (items 1 and 3), no `w(σ)` recovers global structure. Treat this as a knob behind the representation/conditioning fixes.

The principled endpoint that dissolves the whole currency question is EDM (Karras et al. 2022): the `c_skip`/`c_out`/`c_in`/`c_noise` preconditioning normalizes the network target so the loss weight `λ(σ)` *is* the effective weight — no hidden `1/SNR`, no x0-vs-v ambiguity. Pairing EDM preconditioning with its σ-sampling is a cleaner fix than bolting P2 onto an x0 head.

**8. No EMA of weights.** You EMA the *loss* for logging but sample from the raw last-iterate parameters. Weight EMA is close to mandatory for diffusion sample quality (Song & Ermon 2020; Nichol & Dhariwal 2021; a first-class hyperparameter in Karras 2022). Cheapest high-impact fix on this list.

A few concrete bugs worth fixing while you're in there: in `save_dales_pc`, `intensity = features_np[:, 1]` reads the first *class* probability, not intensity (intensity is index 0 of the `features` slice), and the comment says 10 channels where the slice is 9 — your diagnostic exports are mislabeled. In `trilinear_upsample`, `features /= mask` and `global_offset /= mask` divide by an interpolated mask that can be ~0 or negative — numerical landmines on the upsampler path.

## C. Minimal path that actually moves you to dataset-regime generation

If I were prioritizing for fastest signal, in order:

1. **Fix noise conditioning first (item 5).** Bump `time_emb` to ≥128, push it through a 2-layer MLP, and inject it as FiLM in every block. This is the highest-ROI mechanical change and is prerequisite for *anything* population-scale to work. Pair with weight EMA (item 8); for the loss weighting (item 7) prefer **P2-true** (`w_x0 = SNR·(k+SNR)^(−γ)`) or **EDM preconditioning** over Min-SNR-γ for this x0-head — but see item 7's correction, and treat reweighting as second-order behind the representation fixes (items 1–3). Together these are a few hundred lines and routinely make the difference between noise and samples.

2. **Decide your structure representation deliberately (items 1–2).** Either commit to the XCube pattern — diffuse a clean continuous latent on the coarse grid and predict occupancy/subdivision with a dedicated head/loss, keeping occupancy out of the Gaussian channels — or, if you want to stay closer to your current code, at minimum supervise mask with a proper occupancy objective (BCE on logits with the empty/occupied imbalance handled) and treat labels with analog-bits or a small categorical-diffusion head rather than Gaussian-channel + CE-readout. This is what converts "decoupled geometry and labels" into one generative state.

3. **Train on the layout distribution you sample from (item 3).** Move level-0 training off occupancy-centered clips onto full (or uncentered) coarse tiles, even if you must shrink batch or sparsify to afford it. You cannot learn p(layout) from windows that are by construction centered on occupancy.

4. **Then wire `cond` (item 4)** and bring the GVAE in via FiLM/cross-attention + CFG. With 1–3 done, conditioning will actually bite; without them it won't, because a σ-blind, patch-trained, occupancy-as-regression model has no global state for the condition to steer.

The honest one-line version: your *backbone* is now adequate, but your *generative model* is still specified like a single-exemplar resynthesizer — Gaussian-everything state with occupancy as a regressed channel, near-blind noise conditioning, and training windows centered on the very structure you're trying to generate. Fix the noise conditioning and the structure/label representation first; those two are what stand between you and a real generator.
